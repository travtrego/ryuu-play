A quick guide to the rules this game uses. Play a game against a bot from the Games page, and come back here whenever something on the table doesn't make sense.

How to win

You win the moment any one of these happens:

  • You take all 6 of your Prize cards. You take a Prize each time you Knock Out one of your opponent's Pokémon (some powerful Pokémon, like Pokémon-ex, give up 2).
  • Your opponent has no Pokémon left in play. If their Active Pokémon is Knocked Out and they have nothing on the Bench to replace it, you win.
  • Your opponent can't draw. If a player has no cards left in their deck at the start of their turn, they lose.
 Setting up
  • Each player shuffles a 60-card deck and draws 7 cards.
  • Each player puts one Basic Pokémon face down as their Active Pokémon, and may put up to 5 more Basic Pokémon on their Bench.
  • Each player sets aside the top 6 cards of their deck, face down, as Prize cards.
  • A coin flip decides who goes first.
  • No Basic Pokémon in your opening hand? That's a mulligan: you show your hand, shuffle it back and draw 7 again. Your opponent may draw 1 extra card each time this happens.
 Your turn

1. Draw a card. (In this game the player going first draws on their first turn too.)

2. Do any of these, in any order:

  • Put Basic Pokémon from your hand onto your Bench (5 at most).
  • Evolve your Pokémon.
  • Attach one Energy card from your hand to one of your Pokémon. Only one per turn.
  • Play Trainer cards (only one Supporter per turn).
  • Retreat your Active Pokémon, once per turn.
  • Use Abilities, Poké-Powers and other powers printed on your Pokémon.

3. Attack, or end your turn. Attacking always ends your turn, so do everything else first.

Tip: the most common beginner mistake is attacking before attaching Energy or playing Trainers. Attack last.

 Energy and attacking
  • Each attack shows an Energy cost next to its name. Your Active Pokémon needs at least that much Energy attached to use it.
  • A specific symbol (Fire, Water...) needs that exact type. The plain star symbol is Colorless and can be paid with any type of Energy.
  • Using an attack does not use up the Energy. It stays attached unless the attack's text says to discard it.
  • Only your Active Pokémon can attack, and it attacks your opponent's Active Pokémon unless the attack says otherwise.
 Evolving
  • Put a Stage 1 card on the Basic Pokémon it says it "Evolves from", and a Stage 2 card on the matching Stage 1.
  • You can't evolve a Pokémon on the same turn it came into play. That also means neither player can evolve on their very first turn.
  • The evolved Pokémon keeps its attached Energy and the damage it already has, but gets the new card's HP and attacks.
 Retreating
  • To swap your Active Pokémon with one on your Bench, discard Energy from it equal to its Retreat Cost (shown at the bottom of the card).
  • You can retreat once per turn.
  • An Asleep or Paralyzed Pokémon can't retreat.
  • A Pokémon that goes to the Bench is cured of all Special Conditions.
 Damage, Weakness and Resistance
  • Damage stays on a Pokémon. When its total damage reaches its HP, it is Knocked Out: it and all cards attached to it go to the discard pile, and the attacker's owner takes a Prize card.
  • If your Active Pokémon is Knocked Out, you choose a Benched Pokémon to become your new Active Pokémon.
  • Weakness: if the attacker's type matches the defender's Weakness, the damage is doubled (×2). A few cards add a fixed amount instead (for example +20).
  • Resistance: if the attacker's type matches the defender's Resistance, the damage is reduced by the number shown (for example -30).
  • Weakness and Resistance apply to damage done to the Active Pokémon. Damage to Benched Pokémon ignores them.
 Special Conditions

These only affect an Active Pokémon. "Between turns" means right after each player's turn ends.

Poisoned Takes 10 damage between turns.
Burned Between turns, flip a coin. If tails, it takes 20 damage.
Asleep Can't attack or retreat. Between turns, flip a coin. If heads, it wakes up.
Paralyzed Can't attack or retreat. It recovers at the end of its owner's next turn.
Confused When it tries to attack, flip a coin. If tails, the attack fails, it puts 30 damage on itself, and your turn ends. It can still retreat.

Retreating or evolving a Pokémon cures its Special Conditions.

 Trainer cards
  • Items (and plain "Trainer" cards in older sets): play as many as you like, then discard them.
  • Supporters: usually stronger, but only one per turn.
  • Stadiums: stay in play and affect both players. Only one Stadium can be in play; a new one replaces it.
  • Pokémon Tools: attach to one of your Pokémon and stay there.
  • Always read the card: Trainers are how you draw cards, search your deck and heal.
 Beginner tips
  • Fill your Bench early. If your Active Pokémon is Knocked Out with an empty Bench, you lose on the spot.
  • Attach Energy every turn. It's free, and falling behind on Energy is the fastest way to lose.
  • Power up a backup. Put Energy on a Benched Pokémon so it can attack right away when your Active one goes down.
  • Count the damage. Before attacking, check whether you can Knock Out the Defending Pokémon this turn, including Weakness.
  • Watch your deck size. Running out of cards to draw is a loss too, so don't over-use draw cards late in the game.
